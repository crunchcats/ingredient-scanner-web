Privacy policy

Last updated 7 August 2026

The short version: the photo never leaves your phone, and we never learn what you scanned. The app sends a small amount of technical information so it can be kept working, plus one optional thing that you turn on yourself. The detail is below.

Who is responsible

Bloodsugar Guide is provided by GF Consulting ApS, Lykkesholms Allé 11, 1902 Frederiksberg C, Denmark. We are the data controller for the limited information described in this policy. You can reach us at blodsukkerguiden@gmail.com.

What happens on your phone

When you point the camera at an ingredients list, the text is recognised by your own phone, using the text recognition built into iOS and Android. The image is handled on the device. It is not uploaded, not stored by us, and not sent anywhere.

The ingredient list itself is built into the app, so looking something up does not require a network request or an internet connection. We keep no history of your scans, because we never receive them in the first place.

What the app sends

Updates to the ingredient list

The app checks whether a newer version of the ingredient list is available. The list is a single file hosted on GitHub Pages. We do not run a server of our own, and we receive no logs of these requests at all.

The request carries no identifier: no account, no device ID, no advertising ID. As the host, GitHub handles the request technically and can see the IP address it came from and the time it arrived, under its own privacy statement. We never see any of it, and there is nothing in the request that says anything about you or what you scanned.

Crash reports

If the app crashes, a report is sent through Sentry so the fault can be found and fixed. A report contains the device model, the operating system version, the app version, and a technical trace of what the code was doing when it stopped. It does not contain the photo, the recognised text, or anything about the product you were looking at.

Usage statistics

Apple and Google give us aggregated statistics about installations and use of the app — for example how many people opened it in a given week. These are sums across everyone, carry no identifier, and cannot be traced back to a person or a phone. This is standard for every app on their stores and we cannot switch it off.

Names of ingredients we do not recognise — only if you turn it on

If the app meets an ingredient that is not in the list, it can send that single word so it can be looked up and added. This is switched off unless you choose to turn it on, and you can turn it off again at any time in the app's settings.

Only the word itself is sent. Never the photo, never the product, never the rest of the ingredients, and nothing identifying you or your phone. The words are collected on your phone and sent as a batch, so the time you scanned something cannot be worked out from the time we receive it.

What we never collect

  • Your name, email address, or phone number. The app has no account and no sign-up.
  • Photos, or the text read from them.
  • A history of what you have scanned.
  • Your location.
  • Your contacts, calendar, or data from Apple Health or Google Fit.
  • Advertising identifiers. There are no adverts and no third-party advertising trackers in the app.

We do not sell data and we do not share it for marketing.

Our legal basis

Under the GDPR, crash reports rest on our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and fixing faults (Article 6(1)(f)). Sending the names of unrecognised ingredients rests on your consent (Article 6(1)(a)), which you give by turning the setting on and can withdraw at any time by turning it off.

Who else is involved

Sentry (Functional Software, Inc.) processes crash reports on our behalf. GitHub, Inc. hosts the ingredient list file on GitHub Pages. Apple and Google distribute the app and provide the store statistics described above. Each acts as a processor or host under contract and may not use the information for its own purposes.

Transfers outside the EU

GitHub and Sentry are US companies, so the limited technical information described above may be processed outside the EU and EEA. Both provide contractual safeguards for such transfers, based on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses. Nothing about what you scan is included in any of it.

How long anything is kept

  • Requests for the ingredient list: we keep no logs of these at all, because we do not run the server.
  • Crash reports: 90 days.
  • Names of unrecognised ingredients: kept until they have been reviewed. What remains afterwards is the ingredient in the public list, which is not connected to whoever sent it.

Your rights

You have the right to ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal data we hold about you, to object to how we process it, and to withdraw consent you have given.

In practice we hold almost nothing that can be linked to an individual, which is deliberate. That also means we usually cannot find data belonging to a particular person, because there is no identifier to search by. You are welcome to contact us at blodsukkerguiden@gmail.com regardless. If you are unhappy with how we handle it, you can complain to Datatilsynet, the Danish Data Protection Agency, at datatilsynet.dk.

Children

The app is not aimed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top will be updated. Any material change will be shown in the app before it takes effect.
